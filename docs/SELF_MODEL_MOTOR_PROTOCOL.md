# Functional self-model: bounded motor study protocol

Frozen after exploratory seeds 0–3 and before fresh seeds 220–231. This is an experimental host extension, not a recovered Docking Station mechanism or a change to the original DefaultBrain. It tests whether a model of the body's action execution can guide effort for actions selected by the unchanged recovered brain. A structured host intercepts the walking adapter only after the brain has selected a concrete action and target. It does not replace target selection or choose a neural action.

For each seed run the same 540-tick continuous individual under three conditions: normal effort only, effortful execution only, or the optional adaptive self-model choosing between those modes for the same brain-selected movement. Each condition maintains a shadow or active success model and receives only matched execution outcomes for its actual mode; controls do not use its predictions to change effort. Every 32 ticks, the host repositions the same three objects using a seed/tick-determined random layout. It does not reset body position, brain, chemistry, pending action or feedback. Each condition can have different subsequent observations and experiences as a result of its actual actions. The 12 seeds are outside documented prior food-study and development ranges; a bounded scan of explicit seed fields in `runs` and `docs` files under 4 MB found no matches, not proof of global uniqueness.

There are three 180-tick phases: healthy body, concealed impairment, and recovery. In healthy/recovery normal walking steps move 0.35 units; with impairment they move 0.07. Effortful steps use the same brain-selected direction, multiply available strength fivefold, and are capped at 0.35 units. A movement succeeds when measured absolute displacement is at least 0.25; this is an explicit host task criterion and does not establish reaching or consuming a target. The model is not given body strength, phase or food value. It receives action identity, observable context `walking`, offered execution modes, and matched observed action outcome and cost. Its prediction is recorded before the movement. The existing experimental approach/touch executor and action interruption rules still apply.

The physical host maintains a finite separate motor reserve initialized to 100 units; a normal attempt consumes 0.01 and an effortful attempt 0.10. An attempt with insufficient reserve does not move and is canceled as unknown. This is an isolated accounting resource, not Docking Station physiology or the brain's chemical energy. Even 540 maximal-cost attempts consume at most 54 units; exhaustion is therefore not expected in this protocol. The model's fixed selection criterion is predicted success minus 0.25 times its learned cost, with periodic bounded exploration. Evaluation reports realized success minus 0.25 times cost per attempt and per tick, total cost and final reserve; the coefficient is a declared illustrative tradeoff, not a physiological conversion or validated user preference.

Known collisions at the room boundary or an externally scheduled obstacle (tick modulo 53 equals 17) cause no movement and are recorded as an environmental block. Such an attempt is canceled rather than trained as a motor failure; evidence before and after cancellation is checked. Actual target-loss events are recorded from the existing lifecycle and do not generate motor observations unless a movement was attempted. This test relies on the host explicitly reporting collision cause; it does not show that the creature can infer an unseen obstruction from ambiguous feedback. Unattributed or exhausted actions are likewise unknown, not failed competence labels.

Record per-tick brain choices, targets, lifecycle and outcomes; per attempted movement record mode, predicted probability before action, measured displacement, cost, reserve, result and reason. At tick 450 serialize the entire brain/individual, body strength, obstacle state, model evidence and pending state to the separate portable schema. Compare the next 90 ticks with a reconstructed in-process clone. Separate focused tests cover process-independent serialization, action-result matching, misleading/unrelated feedback and pending observations. This study's checkpoint comparison itself is in-process, not an OS-restart proof; the previously qualified baseline process-restart tests remain separate.

Report per-seed phase rates, first and last 45-tick windows, successful movements, progress per tick, costs, reserve, selected effort frequency, utility, false failure predictions, and pre-action Brier scores and average predictions against realized outcomes. Probability scores are on-policy: the three conditions execute different mode distributions, so their scores are not controlled comparisons of counterfactual predictions. The constant 0.5 reference has Brier score 0.25 on binary outcomes. Resample paired seeds 10,000 times with deterministic seed 20261107 and report percentile 95% intervals for adaptive minus normal and adaptive minus always-effort. These intervals quantify the seeded task, not broader claims.

The useful-behavior benchmark is considered supported if impaired adaptive success exceeds normal by a paired seed 95% interval above zero, and adaptive healthy-phase cost is lower than always-effort by a paired seed 95% interval below zero. Report actual magnitudes, success and progress as well as cost-adjusted utility, without hiding recovery lag or missed attempts. No numerical gate was predeclared for recovery speed or calibration; those measurements are descriptive. Initial development outcomes suggested improvement under impairment and slow return to economical effort after recovery. Fresh results, including failures, must be retained without parameter tuning.
