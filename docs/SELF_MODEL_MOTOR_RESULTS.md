# Functional self-model: motor experiment results

The experimental self-model improved movement after a hidden, simulated walking impairment, while spending less than an always-effortful body in the initial healthy phase. It also recovered economical effort slowly after the body returned to normal. These conclusions concern execution of brain-selected movements in this host, not general action competence, food preference, navigation success or native Docking Station parity.

The [frozen protocol](SELF_MODEL_MOTOR_PROTOCOL.md) specified 12 fresh seeds (220–231), three paired conditions, and 180 ticks each of healthy, impaired and recovered body physics. All 36 cases completed. The brain still selects each action and target; adaptive control selects only whether to use normal or costly effort to execute that same movement. Normal and always-effort controls retain shadow predictors but do not use those predictions to select effort. Movement success means observed displacement of at least 0.25 units. The 91.48% figure is the mean of seed-level rates conditional on eligible attempted movements; pooled counts were 1,231 successes among 1,345 adaptive attempts in the impaired phase (91.52%). Brain decisions that did not attempt movement and 505 known environmental blocks across the full study are excluded from this denominator. The experiment also reports utility per tick so changes in action frequency affect that metric. A successful step does not establish that an entire action completed or that its target was reached.

The paired seed bootstrap interval for adaptive minus normal impaired success was +91.48 percentage points [90.62, 92.46]. Adaptive minus always-effort healthy cost was -8.588 units [-9.330, -7.927]. Both predeclared useful-behavior comparisons passed. Adaptive impaired utility per tick exceeded normal by 0.5571 [0.5187, 0.5942], but was below always-effort by 0.0511 [-0.0575, -0.0448]. The 0.25 cost coefficient is an illustrative fixed tradeoff, not a validated biological or owner preference. Per-tick progress during impairment averaged 0.0445 normal, 0.2032 adaptive and 0.2178 always effort; action propensity and trajectories can diverge between conditions.

Adaptive effort remained 80.47% of eligible attempts in the last 45 recovery ticks. Although the simulated body had returned to normal, it had not reliably resumed the economical mode by the end of this test. That is a measured adaptation limitation, not evidence of rapid recovery. The separate 100-unit reserve is host-side accounting rather than brain chemistry; the lowest final reserve was 61.5, so exhaustion and its longer-term effects were not tested.

Adaptive, on-policy Brier scores were 0.0482 healthy, 0.0594 impaired and 0.0352 recovered; a constant 0.5 forecast has score 0.25 for binary outcomes. Mean adaptive predicted success versus observed success was 0.803 versus 1.000 healthy, 0.826 versus 0.915 impaired, and 0.831 versus 1.000 recovered. These forecasts are systematically conservative in the successful phases. Since each condition executes different modes, these are on-policy scores and do not establish equal-input or counterfactual predictive superiority. In recovery, 18 adaptive successful steps were predicted below 0.5, versus 127 in the normal-only shadow model; these rates also depend on which modes were executed.

All 36 in-process checkpoint continuations matched across the cloned brain, body, model, actions and events. The portable checkpoint includes actual body strength, obstacle state, motor reserve, model evidence and pending action state. This study did not itself use a process restart. A separate focused real-process test saved and restored a trained model, body and brain, then matched an eight-tick continuation; the earlier baseline OS-restart checks and self-model pending-state JSON tests provide additional, separate evidence. The 254 scheduled obstacle and 251 boundary-blocked movements were canceled as unknown with the model's outcome evidence unchanged. This depends on the host explicitly reporting the collision. It does not demonstrate correct causal attribution when an unseen obstruction and motor impairment are indistinguishable.

The [raw cases](../runs/self-model/motor-heldout-cases.jsonl) retain per-tick neural choices and outcomes plus pre-action predictions, attempted mode, movement, energy cost and explicit reason. Every one of 12,737 movement records matched that tick's current brain choice ID and target, and the stored probability matched its prediction record. A later audit found that 288 records belonged to continued eating actions whose original executing lifecycle action ID differed from the current choice ID (the targets matched). Those outcomes were matched to the current-choice motor sub-attempt, not the original lifecycle action ID, so full original-action attribution was not established in the frozen run. The frozen source is preserved at `runs/self-model/frozen-motor-benchmark.py`; a later host correction records both IDs and uses a unique motor attempt ID. The [summary](../runs/self-model/motor-heldout-summary.json) contains paired seed-level bootstrap intervals; the [manifest](../runs/self-model/motor-heldout-manifest.json) pins the protocol, model, body, analysis and program digests, which matched after the run. Total reported case CPU was 728.27 seconds; process-family peak memory was not measured. The earlier food-retention and failed reversal results are unchanged.

An independent rerun of the adaptive condition for seed 220 exactly matched all retained choices, observations, model predictions and outcomes after excluding timing fields. The optional [interactive demonstration](../examples/persistent_individual/README.md) uses a separate experimental checkpoint; the original live individual remains on the baseline. A focused suite of 33 model, motor-host, real-process-server, baseline, food and replay tests passed. The experimental server was also stopped and reopened with the same identity, body strength and stored model state. Its reserve-recharge control is a user-directed host operation and was not used in the frozen benchmark.

A later [six-pair attribution follow-up](SELF_MODEL_ATTRIBUTION_FOLLOWUP_RESULTS.md) reproduced the original records on two prior seeds, checked corrected action IDs and targets, and observed unchanged behavior in those cases; it does not replace the original held-out data or claim universal equivalence.
