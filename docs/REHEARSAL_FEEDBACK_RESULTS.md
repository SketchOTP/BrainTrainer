# V18: real outcomes revise rehearsed waiting plans

Earlier executives always rehearsed four held actions before probing again after
repeated ineffective retreat. The goals retained dreams and later progress, but
had no explicit assessment connecting a real probe to its dreamed plan and no
adaptation of that waiting interval.

V18 records a pending review with the dream event, planned interval and target.
After the held actions, a matching, observable real retreat probe evaluates the
plan. A failed probe increases the next interval from four to eight actions;
further failures keep it at eight. Observed recovery resets it to four. Each
review retains its real outcome event and unknown failure cause. Subsequent
dreams cite the real review that changed their interval. Missing observations,
an unrelated outcome, or a different target cannot teach a new interval.

Dreams remain hypothetical: they add no real outcomes, do not move world time,
and cannot teach recovery by themselves. The next interval is retained through
history replay. Prior executive versions retain their four-action semantics.
These are authored learning rules and bounds, not general generative dreaming.

## Tradeoff evaluation

The first candidate allowed waits up to sixteen. It reduced failed probes but
in the 36-tick impairment case did not detect recovery until fifteen ticks later.
That candidate source, checks and full probe checkpoints are preserved under
validation/rehearsal-feedback/initial-16/. It was never installed in the live
individual. The final candidate caps the wait at eight to reduce that delay.

The paired probe uses actual adverse-experience history and explicit rural test
branches. The environment changes body strength; that setting is not supplied to
the executive. It compares observed failed probes during 8, 16, 24 and 36 impaired
ticks, then observes real movement after recovery. These are four recovery-time
conditions from one prepared history, not diverse-personality or generalization
qualification. Every case must demonstrate at least three recovered retreat
steps and exact snapshot restoration. The broader checks include a real child
process resuming a pending rehearsed plan.

This is a behavioral tradeoff, not a general speed or resource benchmark. Fewer
failed probes are useful during persistent impairment; a longer interval can
still delay noticing recovery. There is no claim of uniformly better recovery
latency, learned physical cause, or unlimited adaptation.

## Final results and adoption

Twenty-three focused and related checks passed in 23.518 seconds. Final paired results:

| Impaired ticks | V17 failed probes | V18 failed probes | V17 recovery delay | V18 recovery delay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 9 |
| 16 | 5 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| 24 | 7 | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| 36 | 9 | 7 | 2 | 8 |

Delays are actual ticks until first observed recovered retreat. The candidate reduced futile probes in three conditions, and detected recovery later in two. Both versions demonstrated at least three recovered retreat steps within each twenty-tick recovery window. Results and complete checkpoints are in validation/rehearsal-feedback/paired-results.json and developmental-executive-v*-*.json.

Live preflight at tick 10952 preserved exact body, lineage and prior events and round-tripped through restore. The same individual adopted V18 at tick 10974 using the exclusive store owner and portable before/candidate images. The one-time service hook was removed, running intent restored, and subsequent advancing ticks verified without an error. This is an externally authored mechanism update; the adaptation evidence belongs to explicit test branches. The ordinary live individual still has zero recorded rehearsals.
