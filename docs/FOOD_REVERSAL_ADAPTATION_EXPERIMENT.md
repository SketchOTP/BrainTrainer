# Reversal adaptation: exploratory feedback-gain tests

The held-out gene-7 ablation showed positive but sub-10-point reversal-evaluation differences. Previous [reversal diagnostics](FOOD_REVERSAL_DIAGNOSTICS.md) found incomplete sampling in some branches, reduction of the measured old-food weight sum and increase of the new-food sum in many branches, and no universal ordering in which choice simply lagged a weight crossing. The [targeted feedback replay](FOOD_INDEPENDENT_REPLAY.md) observed a correctly attributed new-good outcome entering response and drive neurons alongside tract changes; it did not isolate the outcome as the cause of every change. These findings do not identify a single established cause of weak reversal.

## Intervention and design

The controlled food environment routes a requested hunger change of `-0.25` for beneficial food and `+0.125` for harmful food. This sign/magnitude asymmetry is an **environment and host-interface fact**, not a claim about native Docking Station. `WorseningFeedbackGainExperiment` tests a minimal optional host-interface adjustment: multiply an already-attributed, positive hunger-training response by a fixed gain, capped at 1. It does not change measured energy, hunger, chemistry, action execution, target selection, or the recovered brain kernel. Negative responses are unchanged. It has no access to hidden value labels or reversal phase. This gain is a design choice and can alter transient neural activity, not just persistent learning.

Two sequential development diagnostics used the same four previously studied seeds 0–3, both bodies, both mirrors and both value assignments: 32 paired contexts per comparison, each with four phases of 20 opportunities and a 32-tick limit. The first tested gain 2; after seeing its negative result, the second tested gain 0.5, reusing the first run's hashed baseline cases only after verifying the program and baseline-source digests. Both are exploratory and involve repeated use of development data. Each body/condition/phase has 320 opportunities from 16 correlated branches across four seeds; branches are not independent. A success is a completed consumption of the currently higher-value food; non-completions remain in the denominator. Values are not exposed to the brain.

| Body and phase | Recovered baseline | Gain 2 | Gain 0.5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Walking, initial evaluation | 115/320 (35.9%) | 115/320 (35.9%) | 115/320 (35.9%) |
| Walking, reversal experience | 50/320 (15.6%) | 27/320 (8.4%) | 60/320 (18.8%) |
| Walking, reversal evaluation | 49/320 (15.3%) | 39/320 (12.2%) | 48/320 (15.0%) |
| Swimming, initial evaluation | 114/320 (35.6%) | 114/320 (35.6%) | 114/320 (35.6%) |
| Swimming, reversal experience | 36/320 (11.3%) | 20/320 (6.3%) | 60/320 (18.8%) |
| Swimming, reversal evaluation | 44/320 (13.8%) | 22/320 (6.9%) | 46/320 (14.4%) |

Gain 2 was counterproductive on the reversal phases in this small sample. For gain 0.5, the paired seed-level mean gain-minus-baseline difference during reversal experience was +3.13 percentage points walking and +7.50 points swimming. The later reversal-evaluation differences were −0.31 and +0.63 points; their descriptive paired bootstrap intervals included zero. Initial evaluation scores were identical, but this does **not** mean all initial histories or neural states were identical: 26 of 32 baseline/half-gain pairs had exactly equal pre-reversal event histories, and only 8 of 32 had identical gene-7 tract checkpoints at opportunity 40. The bootstrap resamples only four development seeds and was not used as a confirmatory test.

During reversal, the half-gain condition completed 86 versus 75 walking interactions and 85 versus 59 swimming interactions. Its higher-value completions were 60 versus 50 and 60 versus 36; formerly valuable completions were 26 versus 25 and 25 versus 23. It also produced more eat-action ticks. These are selected and time-correlated activity counts, not independent observations or proof that the gain improved a stored association. The weaker response might affect momentary action selection, persistent state, or both. Of 16 correlated branches per body, six baseline branches had no new-good completion during reversal, versus three with half gain. Among branches with a completion, the median opportunity was 3 versus 3 walking and 4 versus 9 swimming; these conditional medians exclude censored branches and do not establish faster learning.

The half-gain setting is therefore a **candidate for a fresh, preregistered evaluation of early reversal behavior**, not a demonstrated general improvement and not a replacement for the reference brain. In particular, the later evaluation did not show a consistent gain, and prior held-out seeds 56–95 were not used in these tuning experiments. Test matched feedback replays, misleading or unrelated events, initial learning, reversal, checkpoint retention and genuinely fresh independent seeds before any promotion. Do not infer that reducing an aversive signal will help in other bodies, reward scales or environments.

## Evidence and validation

The gain-2 raw cases and recovered summaries are `runs/food-qualification/worsening-feedback-gain-dev-v1.cases.jsonl`, `...-v1.json` and `...-v1-verified.json`. The producer encountered a summary-only variable-shadowing error after all 64 raw records were written. Its exact [producer source](../runs/food-qualification/worsening-feedback-gain-dev-v1-producer.py) and [gain-2 extension source](../runs/food-qualification/worsening-feedback-gain-dev-v1-extension.py) were preserved and their digests match the recovered report. The original raw file has a post-run digest, not a pre-run cryptographic receipt. The intermediate recovery-summary script was subsequently extended to run gain 0.5 and its exact earlier source was not separately retained; the current-source reanalysis independently reproduced the same metrics. All 64 expected keys, paired initial brain hashes/observations/first choices, phase lengths and checkpoint continuation flags were checked.

The half-gain [summary](../runs/food-qualification/worsening-feedback-gain05-dev-v1.json) and its corresponding `...cases.jsonl` include the baseline file and report digests, raw per-tick history, gene-7 snapshots at phase boundaries, per-seed differences and checkpoint checks. The half-gain run used approximately 462 seconds wall and 3,594 new worker CPU seconds; maximum cumulative per-worker peak RSS is recorded in the summary, not process-family peak memory. The combined CPU total in that report includes reused baseline cases. The gain-2 run used about 7,410 summed case CPU seconds; its original full-run wall time was not recovered.

Focused tests check gain-1 parity with the original lifecycle, positive and negative response routing, feedback-withheld behavior, rejection of a mismatched target, checkpoint continuation and summary recovery. The final full local unittest suite passed **274 tests**. The ordinary lifecycle, brain kernel, native evidence and prior held-out artifacts remain unchanged. To restore the experimental extension use `WorseningFeedbackGainExperiment.restore`; legacy ordinary loaders do not apply its gain automatically.
