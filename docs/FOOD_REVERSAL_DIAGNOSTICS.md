# Reversal diagnostics for the in-contact development comparison

These observations are from the existing reconstructed-brain development experiment, not a new native run or held-out test. Sixteen seeds each contributed four mirrored and counterbalanced branches per body and condition: **64 branches are not 64 independent seeds**. Counts describe those branches, not general population probabilities. Source file hashes and per-branch records are retained in [the diagnostic artifact](../runs/food-qualification/gene7-reversal-diagnostics-v1.json).

The analysis derives the two food/eat combination cells from the existing `comb→attn` and `comb→decn` tracts: food categories 3 and 8 and eat decision 4. It reconstructs gene-7 dendrite state by applying each recorded per-tick delta to the case's initial tract. The reported weight is the sum of dendrite variable 0 for drive neuron 2 and a selected food/eat cell; it is a narrow, signed state measure, not a proven stand-alone food-value readout. A change in an endpoint count is distinct from a change in that weight sum.

During the 20 reversal-experience opportunities, the ordinary condition requested eating the newly valuable type in 59/64 walking and 59/64 swimming branches, and completed at least one such consumption in 53/64 and 51/64 respectively. Thus 11/64 and 13/64 branches never completed a newly valuable item during this period; insufficient sampling remains plausible for those branches. Requests are counted per brain tick, not as independent sampling attempts. Across all ordinary branches, there were 266 newly valuable and 82 formerly valuable consumptions for walking, and 226 and 79 for swimming. These counts include repeated interactions and cannot be treated as independent observations.

On the very first reversal opportunity, ordinary branches successfully consumed the newly valuable food in 3/64 walking and 1/64 swimming cases; the frozen figures were 10/64 and 7/64. This is consistent with an initial carryover from the previous mapping, but the opportunity ends can also be non-completions and cannot independently establish its cause.

The recorded pending queue shows 253 walking and 206 swimming newly valuable outcome events delivered during reversal ticks in the ordinary condition. Every one coincided with a recorded gene-7 dendrite change. The count excludes feedback delivered after the reversal phase boundary, and ordinary gene-7 dendrites also changed on many ticks without such feedback. The trace records planned delivery through the adapter's response route, not the resulting response-neuron state. Temporal coincidence therefore does not establish that the feedback was effective, caused those changes, or created an association.

Comparing immediately before and after reversal experience, the ordinary drive-2→old-food/eat variable-0 sum decreased in 51/64 walking and 48/64 swimming branches; the corresponding new-food/eat sum increased in 50/64 and 51/64. Both changes occurred together in 37 and 35 branches, respectively. Newly valuable endpoint count increased in 14/64 and 13/64 branches; old endpoint count decreased in none. Across all reversal ticks, 16 walking and 15 swimming gene-7 dendrite endpoint migrations were recorded. Thus the observed changes are often weight changes on retained endpoints, while a smaller number of branches acquire additional matching endpoints. This is descriptive: the signs, sums and endpoint classifications do not identify an association's native causal meaning.

The frozen control recorded no persistent gene-7 changes by construction. In that condition, 46/64 walking and 49/64 swimming branches completed the newly valuable food at least once during reversal. Ordinary and frozen conditions generate different later sensory histories and outcomes, so these branch counts should not be read as a controlled estimate of the cause of sampling differences.

For ordinary walking, the median first newly valuable consumption was reversal opportunity 4 among the 53 branches that had one; for swimming it was opportunity 8 among 51. These medians exclude censored branches and are secondary diagnostics. The first successful choice after the first delivered new-good feedback occurred in 45/64 walking and 40/64 swimming branches; this is temporal order, not attribution. The accompanying lag analysis tests a narrower numerical weight-order crossing and records whether a newly good request or successful completion followed it. It does not equate that crossing with a learned preference.

The [lag artifact](../runs/food-qualification/gene7-reversal-lag-v1.json) recorded the first reversal tick when the drive-2/new-food/eat variable-0 sum exceeded the corresponding old-food sum. This crossing occurred in 59/64 ordinary walking and 56/64 ordinary swimming branches. Of those crossing branches, a successful newly valuable consumption occurred strictly in an earlier opportunity in 22/59 and 20/56, and within the same opportunity in 20/59 and 20/56; a later successful consumption was also observed in 51/59 and 47/56. These categories can overlap over time, and same-opportunity counts do not establish ordering inside that opportunity. Therefore the records show both earlier and later choices relative to this numerical crossing; they do not support a universal claim that choices simply lag connection changes. The crossing is a provisional proxy, not a demonstrated food-value representation. No persistent crossing was recorded under the frozen control.
