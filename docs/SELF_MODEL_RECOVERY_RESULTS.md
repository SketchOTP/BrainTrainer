# Self-model recovery: held-out result

The confirmation candidate passed all three gates in the [frozen protocol](SELF_MODEL_RECOVERY_PROTOCOL.md), for the bounded walking task and sustained body impairment. It reduced late recovery effort while retaining the baseline's measured impairment success and healthy-phase cost. It did not eliminate slow recovery, and an additional noisy-physics scenario exposed a small, measurable loss of movement success during impairment. This is an experimental execution-layer extension, not a recovered Docking Station mechanism or a general self-model of a creature.

The study ran 12 seeds (300–311), four conditions and three scenarios, for 144 cases and 77,760 primary simulation ticks. The original brain chose each action and target. The current model and candidate chose normal or higher effort only for brain-selected movement; normal-only and always-effortful conditions were separate controls. All cases began from matched seeded initial states, but later observations and actions could differ because execution changed the world. The candidate preserved the existing eight-decision probe frequency and scheduled one extra normal confirmation after a matched, surprising normal success with previous normal failure evidence. It did not receive the hidden body-strength schedule.

Percentages in the table are averages of within-seed fractions, not pooled independent trials. Intervals bootstrap the 12 paired seeds, not individual steps. The identical impairment and healthy values reflect this deterministic task and seeded sample; they do not prove a universal lack of tradeoff. In the first 45 ticks after sustained recovery, effort was 86.66% with the current model and 79.24% with confirmation, a paired −7.42 points [−9.12, −5.77]. Over the full 180-tick recovery, effort was 82.88% versus 67.78% and mean motor cost was 9.489 versus 7.922 units. The candidate still used expensive effort on more than half of eligible movements at the end, so recovery was improved but incomplete.

The candidate's benefit is a reduced cost under the predefined motor-cost rules. In the final 45 sustained-recovery ticks, both models succeeded on all 336 eligible movement attempts and made 0.21778 units of progress per tick; none of those 336 attempts was blocked. The illustrative cost-adjusted utility per tick was 0.60921 current versus 0.61304 confirmation, paired +0.00383 [0.00192, 0.00604]. This fixed 0.25 cost coefficient is a design convention, not a biological or owner-valued exchange rate. Fixed normal/always-effort controls both had 100% healthy and recovered eligible movement success, but their healthy motor costs averaged 1.152 and 11.517 units respectively; in sustained impairment, their eligible success rates were 0% and 100%. For comparison, current and candidate success were 90.74% among eligible steps and 87.38% per all attempted steps, counting known blocked attempts in the latter denominator. A successful step means displacement of at least 0.25 units, not that the target was reached or an eat action completed.

The brief-disruption test was separately descriptive. The candidate reduced effort in the first 45 recovery ticks by 1.78 points [0.33, 3.53] compared with the current model, and by 17.95 points [8.06, 29.80] in the final 45 ticks. Both models had the same eligible movement success during the 30-tick impairment in these seeds. No separate gate was declared for this schedule.

The noisy scenario introduced genuinely normal-strength physical ticks on 10% of the impaired phase, determined by seed and tick; these were not forged outcomes or model inputs. During that impairment, eligible step success averaged 91.60% current and 91.12% confirmation: confirmation minus current −0.484 points [−0.843, −0.153]. Per attempted step including blocked attempts, the difference was −0.457 points [−0.802, −0.140]. This is a measurable cost of treating a real but transient normal success as worth checking, and must not be hidden by the sustained-scenario pass. In noisy recovery's final 45 ticks, confirmation reduced effort by 25.67 points [13.09, 41.05], while step success matched in this sample. Neither noisy figure was a preregistered gate.

For sustained recovery, average on-policy success forecasts were 0.828 current and 0.819 confirmation while all eligible steps succeeded; Brier scores were 0.0377 and 0.0418. These are selected-action predictions, not a controlled counterfactual calibration comparison, and the model was conservative in this phase. The uncertainty scores and false-failure counts for every phase and scenario are in the [summary](../runs/self-model/recovery/heldout-summary.json). Environmental blocks are labeled by the host and treated as unknown rather than evidence of motor failure; this does not demonstrate that the model can distinguish hidden obstacles from body impairment without that explicit cause label.

All 144 in-process reconstructed checkpoint continuations matched their uninterrupted counterparts over the final 90 ticks. Separate focused tests use an actual process restart for the confirmation host; this is not a claim that all 144 cases were independently restarted OS processes. An [independent audit](../runs/self-model/recovery/heldout-audit.json) checked all 50,875 motor attempts, including 1,910 known blocked/unknown attempts: action ownership, unique attempt IDs, recorded pre-action predictions, physical schedules and continuation flags matched. The audit rechecked the pre-run manifest source/program hashes. It also avoids repeating the original motor study's current-choice versus executing-action attribution ambiguity.

The [manifest](../runs/self-model/recovery/heldout-manifest.json) pins the exact selected program, protocol, runner, model, typed host and relevant source digests before the fresh study. The [full raw cases](../runs/self-model/recovery/heldout-complete.jsonl) have SHA-256 `0d85d81d80274f18498840b7d0be3995de075609f06a762d74e6ae51547aaa92`; all individual seed shards remain. Reported case CPU sums to 2,653.24 seconds and summed case elapsed time is 2,656.43 seconds across parallel cases; neither is aggregate wall time. Process-family peak memory was not measured. The lowest final reserve was 61.9 of 100, so reserve exhaustion and long-horizon effects were not evaluated.

Development raw results and exact archived candidate and runner sources are recorded in [the development archive receipt](../runs/self-model/recovery/development-archive.json). The twice-as-frequent probing option reduced recovery effort but lost roughly 9.6 percentage points of sustained-impaired success in development; it was not selected. The confirmation option, gates, fresh seeds, scenarios and analysis were frozen before the 144 held-out cases; no held-out tuning was performed. Earlier food-retention, reversal and original motor-study results remain unchanged.

An independent rerun of the noisy-scenario confirmation case for seed 300 reproduced every retained case field except execution timings; its [receipt](../runs/self-model/recovery/independent-replay-seed-300.json) records the runner digest. A focused selection of 49 candidate, control, checkpoint, process-restart, food and replay tests passed. The [optional demonstration](../examples/persistent_individual/README.md) uses a separate confirmation checkpoint and does not replace the live baseline individual; conversion from an adaptive motor checkpoint is explicit and leaves its source unchanged.
