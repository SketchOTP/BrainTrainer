# Familiar activity protocol, v1

Freeze before seeds 121–124. Each prepared parent forks two explicit experimental
histories. In one, object `toy` responds every press and `object-0` every second
press; the other reverses those experiences. Type labels are opaque and stable.
The individual must learn both effects through ordinary selected practice. No
preferred target or activity command is supplied.

After learning, hide both objects for 33 lived ticks, then offer both as ordinary
switches at equal opposing distances. Keep the learned type labels. Observe the
first self-set familiar-activity goal and its actual executed target. It must
prefer the target previously observed to respond more reliably in each history.
Record the comparison of recent outcome evidence that caused the choice.

Then make the preferred target inert, without changing its perceptual type. Over
180 ordinary ticks, require real failed revisits and eventual voluntary return to
the alternative. No direct preference edits are permitted. Save full branch
histories and verify restoration of current goals and review.

The choice mechanism ranks observed visible-effect rates over the latest eight
attributed interaction outcomes. Unknown interactions retain discovery goals;
known harmful interactions are excluded. Familiar returns wait at least 32 ticks
since prior goal-directed practice. The window, interval and preference for visible
responsiveness are authored initial mechanisms. Experience determines rankings;
these are not learned values for all possible kinds of companionship.

Success establishes history-shaped activity preference and revision under changed
outcomes. It does not establish general personality, emotions, attachment or
self-chosen human partners. The existing visible v6 companion remains unchanged.
