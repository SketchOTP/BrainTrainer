# Partner history: development results

The new relationship component selected the expected assistance recipient in all
four phases of all eight natural-outcome histories:

| Phase | Expected behavior | Histories |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Familiar and newcomer available | Prefer repeated helpful history | 8/8 |
| Familiar person observed absent | Ask available newcomer | 8/8 |
| Familiar person's outcomes became harmful | Revise preference to newcomer | 8/8 |
| Both people observed absent | Make no assistance request | 8/8 |

Actor names were swapped independently of their roles. The legacy equal-mean
selection always chose `zuri`, exposing its lexical tie behavior. The new authored
score shrinks sparse histories toward zero and uses recent observed outcomes;
it is not a probability, a moral assessment or an attachment score. Presence and
person/system classification are explicit host observations. The learner does
not infer that a system is a person or that an unknown person is available.

The recovered brain made every world-action decision. Across 88 offered
interactions, 72 completed and 16 did not; the non-completions remain in the
records. In each history two later harmful offers were not completed within the
32-tick window. This panel does not isolate why those actions changed and does
not establish harm avoidance. Hunger, neural state and world time were not reset.

The outcome owner now produces one parsed `ExperiencedRelief` fact for food recall
and partner memory. Consumers do not independently reinterpret raw attribution.
The ledger remains authoritative and reconstructs both projections on restart.
Relationship adoption is explicit, requires learned recall, preserves prior
history, and records the authored implementation change. No creature-directed
software modification is claimed.

## Evidence and limits

- Frozen protocol and source: `validation/partner-development-v1/source/`.
- Raw outcomes and phase requests: `validation/partner-development-v1/cases.jsonl`.
- Independent retained-reference and source-digest audit:
  `validation/partner-development-v1/audit.json`; 24 non-null requests linked to
  observed presence, an attributed offer and an actual positive outcome.
- Related suite: 34 tests passed in 22.627 seconds, zero failures/errors/skips.
  After adding the adoption precondition, all six partner-focused tests passed;
  logs are `validation/partner-tests.log` and
  `validation/partner-final-focused-tests.log`.
- Actual OS-process restart after adoption and experience matched complete
  continuation and intentions.
- Two previously seen recall cases were continued for 12 ticks using the archived
  original and current implementation. Their complete checkpoints matched;
  `validation/partner-legacy-compatibility/receipt.json`. This is bounded
  compatibility evidence, not universal equivalence or a new recall panel.
- Public CLI forked a preserved history, marked its familiar person absent, ran
  one tick, and displayed a grounded request for the available newcomer:
  `validation/partner-public-cli.{json,log}`.

This is a development panel, not an independently held-out relationship
qualification. Requests remain structured intentions exposed through the CLI;
no real person received a message or provided assistance during the experiment.
No recognition, reciprocal play, sustained companionship or enduring attachment
is demonstrated. Presence can become stale without new observations. Broad
activity selection, communication in a visible living world, request lifecycle,
self-knowledge and self-directed executable development remain incomplete.
