# Food benchmark movement and learning-control investigation

This development-only investigation preserves the frozen baseline and its raw results. It does not claim a learned food preference or native action parity. Fresh confirmatory seeds 56–95 remain unused.

## Movement and contact

The v1 adapter makes an eat request a no-op unless the selected food is already within 0.85 units. The [retained native touch investigation](../research/docking-station-20261103/brain-connections-20261106/touch-investigation/REPORT.md) observed the original action using its approach and touch commands before the target food event. This identifies a meaningful implementation difference; it does not establish that the simplified benchmark must reproduce native timing, pathfinding or action interruption.

The [movement/touch audit](../runs/food-qualification/movement-touch-audit-v1.json) examines the four-development-seed diagnostic. In the walking embodied/feedback cell, 217 of 222 eat requests selected a target outside contact and five completed. In swimming, 134 of 138 were outside contact and four completed. The sample counts steps, not independent choices or seeds. It also distinguishes subsequent movement toward/away from the selected target where a following step is observable. It does not establish why the brain selected any action.

`EatApproachProbe` is an **optional body-rule counterfactual**, not a change to the recovered brain or the baseline adapter. On an out-of-contact eat request, it moves one body step toward the same selected target. It never automatically consumes, switches the target, or keeps an action running: the next tick still uses the next brain choice. It deliberately does not claim to emulate the native `APPR`/`TOUC` script.

In [128 paired development cases](../runs/food-qualification/eat-approach-v1.json) (seeds 0–7, both bodies, mirrored layouts and both outcome assignments, eight opportunities per case, 16 ticks per opportunity), initial observation and initial brain choice matched in every paired case. Walking completed 12/256 opportunities under the original immediate-contact rule and 73/256 under the approach-on-eat counterfactual; swimming completed 10/256 and 58/256. These are exploratory pooled completions, not confirmatory learning scores or a test of preference. The additional movement changes later inputs and trajectories. Outcomes varied sharply by seed: for walking four of eight seeds had more completions with the probe, and for swimming three did; three seeds had zero completions under both modes in each body. The raw per-case histories are in `runs/food-qualification/eat-approach-v1.cases.jsonl`. The six-worker run took about 73 seconds wall time and 429 summed worker CPU seconds; maximum cumulative per-worker peak RSS was 19,708 KiB, not total process-family memory.

## Scoped persistent-state control

`freeze_drive_comb_tract_state` executes the normal gene-7 drive-to-combination tract update using private copies of that tract's dendrites, weak queue and helper. Neuron writes and RNG progression remain live; the copied persistent tract state is discarded at the tract boundary. This intentionally prevents persistence of both structural and weight/helper changes in **that one tract**. `DriveCombFrozenExperiment` reapplies the control each tick and marks its own checkpoint; restore through that class to retain the control.

The [controlled replay](../runs/food-qualification/tract-freeze-audit-v1.json) found identical whole-brain state at tract entry and immediately before the private state was discarded, identical first-tick output, and identical post-tick state outside the target tract. The ordinary tract changed while the controlled tract retained its initial state. A JSON checkpoint with the control reapplied continued identically. The [brain-chosen outcome replay](../runs/food-qualification/tract-freeze-brain-event-v1.json) reproduced the same bounded comparison following an actual brain-selected eat and matched food response in the reconstructed environment.

This qualifies a **gene-7 tract persistent-state freeze under the observed replays**, not a whole-brain learning-disabled control. Other tracts and retained neuron state remain live, and future choices can change once discarded state would otherwise have been reused. The conservative [rule-write inventory](../runs/food-qualification/learning-scope-inventory.json) lists possible dendrite, neuron and helper writes and four migratory tracts; it is a static scan, not proof that every listed rule executes or that all learning is identified. A broader learning-disabled claim requires a qualified account of these other pathways. The ordinary brain and v1 checkpoint remain unchanged.

The inventory is not exhaustive: SVRule opcodes 36–41 and 50–51 also write neuron input or activity slots. A [matched replay](../runs/food-qualification/feedback-retained-state-v3.json) starts from the same checkpoint after an actual brain-selected consumption and holds all later sensory frames and chemistry fixed. Its only branch difference is delivery or withholding of the attributed response input. At the first tick, drive neuron 2 slots 3 and 7, response neuron 2 slot 0, and gene-7 endpoint, variable and weak-queue state differ. At the next tick, the feedback branch chooses eat while the withheld branch chooses movement; decision, attention and combination neuron states also differ. At tick eight combination slots 0, 2, 3 and 4, and decision slots 0 and 5 still differ. Only gene 7 differed between tract states in this single eight-tick replay. These observed differences are feedback-sensitive state, not proof that each slot stores a learned association or that other pathways are irrelevant in other circumstances. Freezing all neuron values would suppress transient computation; freezing only the observed tract does not qualify a whole-brain control. No new native execution is implied.

The [small paired tract-ablation comparison](../runs/food-qualification/tract-freeze-food-v1.json) used the optional approach probe on four development seeds, mirrored layouts, both food-value assignments and eight opportunities per run. All 32 pairs began with identical observations, choices and gene-7 tract state; all 32 frozen runs retained that tract state. In walking, ordinary runs completed 29/128 opportunities and the frozen runs 24/128; the corresponding higher-value outcomes were 24 and 12. In swimming, completion was 21/128 and 16/128, with 18 and 8 higher-value outcomes. Later inputs and actions can diverge, and the sample is too small and selected to establish a learned preference or generalize a treatment effect. Raw histories and both initial and final tract states are retained in `runs/food-qualification/tract-freeze-food-v1.cases.jsonl`. Four workers used 53 seconds wall time and 205 summed worker CPU seconds; maximum cumulative per-worker peak RSS was 21,048 KiB, not process-family memory. This is a tract-ablation experiment, not a learning-enabled versus globally learning-disabled benchmark.
