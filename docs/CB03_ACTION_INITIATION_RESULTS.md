# CB-20261107-03: eating initiation and interruption

The CB-02 fresh comparison remains paused. The development data show two distinct bottlenecks: in the 32 paired-controller branches at distance 2.0, 24 did not request eat, four requested it but did not finish, and four completed. The four completions are one seed/layout repeated under four controls, not independent contexts. See the [full decision and dispatch report](CB03_DECISION_DISPATCH_DIAGNOSTIC.md) for all 32 branches, original trace provenance and the host's unsupported alternative-action limits.

Food attention and target resolution were present for the non-requesting baseline branches. Their first missing transition was the raw decision winner: the relevant decision neuron 4 did not win. The recovered mapping converts decision 4 to external eat action/script offset 12; no evidence shows an eat winner lost in mapping or dispatch. In a seed-0 versus seed-1 observational pair, the original 64-tick host traces were reproduced under identical captured neural inputs and chemical frames. At tick 46, seed 1's eat-neuron variable 0 was nonzero but another neuron won. Different seed initialization changes many neural and RNG variables; the pair does not identify an individual causal connection. The host observes relative distance, direction and contact for execution but does not directly pass those fields into neural lobes; no native sensory parity or causal effect is established from that fact.

The successful seed-0 mirror and its failing mirror had identical decision choices through the successful case's 56 ticks. Both first selected eat action 1 and then genuinely selected another action, which the experimental executor treats as an interruption. Both later selected eat again as action 46; the executor retained its original action ID and target despite changing subsequent choice IDs. The failed mirror's target was farther away because of prior movement, and the brain selected another action while the executor was still approaching. At that late start, the remaining opportunity budget was also shorter than the host's minimum physical approach-plus-contact-plus-consume time. This is a separate timing bound, not proof that timing caused the observed decision change. The host's rule for interrupting on a changed brain choice has not been established as native Docking Station parity.

The [readiness and rehearsal report](CB03_READINESS_AND_REHEARSAL.md) documents why CB-01 and CB-02 starts were not equivalent: CB-01 had 20 acquisition presentations and then a two-food in-contact standardized probe at hunger 0.5; CB-02 started with one offered food, hunger 0.8, a fresh brain, and 29 expressed instinct records loaded but not rehearsed. Neither of these particular hosts performed instinct rehearsal. The original [Creatures Genetics Kit manual](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/1838430/manuals/Genetics_Kit_Manual.pdf?t=1640163808) describes sleep-related instinct training and notes that instincts influence rather than guarantee behavior; that source does not establish what this reconstructed host actually did or native sleep timing here.

Exactly three targeted pairs were used: (1) seed-1 fixed-input brain-only baseline versus one-pass boot rehearsal, (2) observational seed-0 versus seed-1 matching frozen input and chemistry frames, and (3) seed-1 ordinary-body baseline versus that rehearsal. In the fixed-input pair the baseline never chose eat in 64 frames; the rehearsed brain chose it in 64 of 64. In the ordinary-body pair the baseline did not request eat within 64 ticks, while the rehearsed brain chose eat and completed consumption at tick 6 without an injected body action or outcome. Its feedback was observed queued and absent on the next normal tick; source code, rather than a queue-call instrument in this pair, establishes the intended routing. These are one-seed findings: rehearsal bundles 29 records, internal tick advance, RNG, neuron and connection changes, so no specific gene or connection is identified. Once actions diverge, actual body and feedback histories also diverge. This is not evidence of native sleep parity or all-seed readiness.

A uniform, explicitly recorded one-pass preparation for every seed and condition is proposed in the readiness report as a **future, unqualified setup**, with no screening on whether the creature later chooses eat and with non-completion retained. The three-pair budget is exhausted. No recovered brain, action mapping, gain or prior result was changed, because these observations establish missing preparation and valid changed decisions but not a proven defect requiring a code fix. Before a renewed qualification, independently freeze and test that preparation across development seeds, distinguish action-selection and host-interruption questions, and preserve the original failed calibration and all unassessed CB-02 gates.
