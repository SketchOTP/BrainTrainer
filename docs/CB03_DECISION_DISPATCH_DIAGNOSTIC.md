# CB-03 decision and interruption trace from the frozen calibration

This report analyzes existing, initially healthy CB-02 calibration data and includes one bounded observational replay. It does not establish native parity. The [machine-readable projection](../runs/self-model/complete-action/cb03-decision-diagnostic.json) contains all 32 branches at the selected 2.0-unit distance: four seeds, two mirrored positions and four execution conditions. Its [producer](../examples/persistent_individual/analyze_cb03_decision.py) pins both its own source and the full calibration file digests. The 64-tick budget includes time before any eat choice; early completion ends event recording but does not change that budget. A first projection used early-stop trace length as the budget and is retained as `cb03-decision-diagnostic-initial-superseded.json`; these conclusions use the corrected projection.

## Attention, decision and dispatch

The recovered mapping table in `src/creature_brain/neural.py` maps decision neuron 4 to external action/script offset 12. In the baseline condition at 2.0 units, all four seeds and both mirrors attended neuron/category 3 and resolved the food target `object-0` on all recorded ticks. For seed 1, the 64 decision winners were neuron 10 (23 ticks), 11 (21), 12 (14), 1 (5) and 3 (1), mapping to actions 9, 10, 11, 1 and 7 respectively. For seed 2, winners 11, 2, 1, 5 and 12 mapped to actions 10, 6, 1, 2 and 11; for seed 3, winners 11, 12, 9 and 2 mapped to actions 10, 11, 8 and 6. No neuron 4 won in any of those six baseline seed/mirror trials, so no eat action was available to dispatch. The first missing transition is the observed decision output, not a dropped eat request. These recorded winners alone cannot say why neuron 4 did not win or whether earlier sensory/combination-lobe representations were sufficient.

In this example host, `observe()` exposes each object's relative position, distance and contact to the executor, but `Individual.tick()` passes visual category and hunger drive into the brain, not those geometry/contact fields as direct neural-lobe inputs. Attributed feedback can also be queued through the response route; neither selected replay delivered any such food feedback. This is a limitation of this reconstructed host contract, not evidence about native Docking Station sensory encoding or proof that geometry omission caused absent eating.

The executor does not implement every competing action for the offered food: in the baseline mirror-false cases it reported `unavailable-action` for 29 ticks in seed 1, 35 in seed 2 and 18 in seed 3. Left/right movement was executed. Those unsupported alternative actions are a separate contract limitation; they do not show that an eat output was dropped in those non-requesting cases.

## Seed 0 mirrored execution

For both mirrors the raw decision winners and mapped actions are identical through the successful trace's first 56 ticks. On zero-based tick 1, neuron 4 selected eat for `object-0`; the executor started action ID `1` and approached. At tick 2 the brain genuinely selected neuron 12/action 11; the host interrupted action `1` and executed the new movement. The interruption follows the experimental host's policy; native parity for a competing brain-selected action remains unverified.

At zero-based tick 46 the brain selected neuron 4/action 12 again, starting original action `46` on `object-0`. The brain continued choosing eat on ticks 47–55. The executor correctly retained original ID `46` and the same target, even though each later brain choice had a new tick-based ID. At that second start, recorded target distance was 3.60 in the successful mirror and 6.90 in the other mirror, after earlier brain-selected motion; the different geometry was not a different decision winner. In the successful mirror, contact was observed at tick 54 and consumption at tick 55. In the other mirror the creature approached to 3.05 units, then at tick 57 the brain selected neuron 11/action 10 and the host interrupted action `46`. This is a genuine changed decision under the host's interruption rule, not an absent target or bookkeeping-only cancellation.

At tick 46 only 18 of the 64 opportunity ticks remained, including the tick on which the second request started. With a maximum 0.35-unit movement and 0.85 contact threshold, a target initially 6.90 units away needs at least 18 movement ticks to reach contact, followed by the host's contact and consumption stages. Thus the available budget also cannot complete this specific second request even if its eat choice were held. This arithmetic is a bound under the example host physics, not evidence that timing caused the observed interruption; no forced-action or latch run is counted as natural success.

## Paired observational replay: seed 0 versus seed 1

One of the authorized maximum three paired replays reconstructed seed 0 and seed 1 under the baseline controller, mirror false and 2.0-unit food placement. No action, learning outcome or target was injected. A wrapper captured the original input, all 256 chemical values, all nonzero queued lobe inputs and post-update decision, stimulus, drive, visual, attention and selected combination states; it then returned the unmodified neural output. Both entire host opportunity records exactly matched the original frozen calibration, and each neural output matched its recorded host choice. The captured external input, chemical and pending-lobe-input frames were identical across the two seeds for all 64 ticks. Different seeds also mean different neural initialization, connectivity and RNG state, so this pair cannot attribute the outcome to one connection or rule.

The food/eat combination cell is 163 as derived from `comb→attn` and `comb→decn` endpoint intersection. At zero-based tick 46, seed 0 decision neuron 4 had variable 0 = 0.003675806 and won, mapping to eat. At the same tick seed 1 neuron 4 had variable 0 = 0.004766183 but neuron 12 won with variable 0 = 0.005842882, mapping to action 11. Across seed 1's 64 ticks, neuron 4 variable 0 was nonzero on five ticks (43–47) but never won. This establishes a competing decision output in this replay; it does not establish that a simple comparison of variable 0 explains all winner-selection behavior. At tick 46 internal `stim` neuron 3 variable 0 was approximately 0.281157 for seed 0 and 0.260105 for seed 1 despite identical supplied frames, showing an internal difference rather than an observed input/chemistry difference. Its specific cause remains unresolved.
